Smart Mirror at HK-Shenzhen Innovation Park (HSITP)

Jan 3, 2024

The Oolaa smart mirror took its place at the Hong Kong-Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park (HSITP) — the 87-hectare innovation hub being built across two phases at the Lok Ma Chau Loop — where Hong Kong's research future is literally under construction.

About the Hong Kong-Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park

HSITP covers an area of 87 hectares, and its development is divided into two phases. The estimated total floor area of the first phase will be 1 million square meters, providing space for Research and Development (R&D) including wet labs and advanced manufacturing facilities.

The park is one of the most significant technology infrastructure projects in the region: a purpose-built district where research talent, laboratories and production sit side by side. Phase one concentrates on R&D capacity — the wet labs and advanced manufacturing space that turn research papers into working prototypes and then into products.

Why an innovation park showcases a smart mirror

A smart mirror is an ideal ambassador for a technology park because it is innovation you can touch. Visitors to HSITP range from researchers to investors to government delegations, and a mirror that responds to voice, displays information and connects to smart home ecosystems communicates in thirty seconds what a brochure cannot.

Innovation parks live or die on demonstration. The technologies that win attention — and funding — are the ones people can interact with on the spot. A smart mirror on display at HSITP does double duty: it shows off Hong Kong-developed product engineering, and it hints at the smart buildings and connected homes the park's own research will eventually fill.

Smart displays in research and demonstration environments

Consider the general scenario of a technology showcase space: a delegation walks through, and instead of a static poster about IoT, they speak to a mirror in Cantonese and watch it pull up information, control connected devices and respond in real time. The demonstration is the product. That immediacy is why interactive displays have become standard equipment in innovation centers worldwide.

The same logic applies to the R&D facilities HSITP is building. Wet labs and advanced manufacturing floors need information surfaces that work with gloved hands and busy workflows — voice control and glanceable displays fit environments where touching a keyboard is impractical. The smart mirror form factor, born for bathrooms and foyers, turns out to be surprisingly at home wherever people need information without breaking stride. For the researchers and engineers who will fill the park's first phase, that kind of ambient, hands-free information access is not a luxury — it is how modern R&D floors are expected to work.

Hong Kong engineering, Shenzhen manufacturing

HSITP's deeper significance for Oolaa is structural: the park embodies the Hong Kong–Shenzhen innovation corridor that the company already lives in. Oolaa is a Hong Kong company (Viewdex Limited) with its own factory in Shenzhen — factory-direct, not a trading company — and 20+ years in the business.

That combination matters for technology buyers. In-house software engineering plus an owned factory means the product on display is the product that ships — no middlemen, no rebadged hardware, and real control over quality and customization. It is the same formula HSITP is trying to scale across the Loop: Hong Kong research and design, Shenzhen manufacturing depth, and products that can stand in front of a global audience. With 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau, Oolaa's smart mirrors have already made that journey from prototype to product — which is exactly the story an innovation park exists to tell.
