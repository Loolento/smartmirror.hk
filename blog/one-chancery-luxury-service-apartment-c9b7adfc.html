One Chancery: Oolaa Smart Mirrors in Luxury Suites

Sep 18, 2026

One Chancery is a new luxury service apartment at 1 Chancery Lane, Central, developed by China Resources Land and set beside Tai Kwun, with accommodation ranging from studios to a four-bedroom penthouse. Oolaa worked with the project's interior designers to craft the smart mirror and vanity configuration in every bathroom — one considered detail in a building where every finish had to earn its place.

Watch the video above for a look inside. In a service apartment, the bathroom is where residents start and end every day; it is also where a property quietly proves whether its "luxury" label is real. A resident may forgive a slow elevator once. The bathroom mirror, used twice a day in dressing-table light, gets no such forgiveness. One Chancery's answer was to treat the mirror as part of the architecture, not as an accessory added at the end.

What defines the One Chancery bathrooms

The palette is calm and precious: white marble walls with grey veining, set off by a gold showerhead, tap and frame. A glass shower enclosure with gold trim completes a hotel-grade finish — the kind of bathroom that photographs well, but more importantly, works well at 7 a.m. on a weekday, when good light and clear information matter more than looks.

The centerpiece is a rounded backlit smart mirror. The soft curve breaks up the straight lines of stone and metal, and the backlight gives the room an even, flattering glow. On its surface, the mirror displays the time, the weather and digital information — the essentials of the morning, readable at a glance, with no phone left balancing on the sink and no news app demanding attention before coffee.

Because Oolaa developed the mirror and vanity configuration together with the interior designers, the technology sits inside the design rather than on top of it. The mirror is sized, lit and positioned as part of the vanity — a piece of the room's composition that happens to be intelligent. That collaboration, from drawings rather than from a catalogue after the fact, is what keeps the technology invisible until the moment it is useful.

How the smart mirror fits service-apartment living

A serviced residence has to satisfy two audiences at once: guests who expect hotel polish, and residents who live there long enough to notice every inconvenience. A smart mirror serves both. It works with iOS and Google smart home platforms and is powered by AI, so the information it shows stays relevant through the day rather than frozen on a welcome screen.

The same configuration is integrated across all units, from the studios to the four-bedroom penthouse. That consistency matters for the operator as much as for the resident: one standard of bathroom, one standard of experience, whichever door you open. Housekeeping, maintenance and future upgrades all get simpler when every bathroom speaks the same language.

As a bathroom mirror, it is also built for the room it lives in — IP65-rated against bathroom moisture, with a built-in defogger and motion detection, so it wakes when a resident walks in and stays clear through a hot shower. It is the quiet, dependable kind of smart: just a mirror that is ready when you are, showing the time and the weather while you get ready to face both.

There is also a rhythm to how the information is used. In the morning the mirror is a briefing: time, weather, the shape of the day. In the evening it settles back into being a mirror — backlit, calm, and part of the room's winding-down ritual. A display that can do both, information when it is wanted and reflection when it is not, fits the peculiar tempo of serviced living, where a unit is at once a hotel room, an office annex and a home. That dual character, more than any single feature, is what the configuration at One Chancery was designed around.

Specifying a bathroom mirror for a serviced residence

For developers and designers working on comparable projects, the One Chancery bathrooms suggest a short checklist. Start with the light: a backlit mirror that flatters faces will be forgiven many things, and one that glares will be forgiven nothing. Match the mirror to the wet environment with a proper IP rating and a defogger, so the surface stays usable after showers. Decide early — with the interior designer, not after the marble is ordered — how the mirror and vanity work together, and standardize the configuration across unit types so the guest experience and the operator's maintenance stay consistent from the smallest studio to the penthouse.
