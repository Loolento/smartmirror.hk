HSITP Dorms Get Oolaa Smart Mirrors at the Foyer

February 04, 2025

The Hong Kong-Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park (HSITP) is a pivotal development in Hong Kong's innovation and technology sector — a site three times larger than the current Hong Kong Science Park, built to house the researchers, engineers, and founders shaping the region's tech future. Oolaa is proud to be part of it: each dormitory at HSITP will be equipped with an Oolaa Smart Mirror at the foyer, allowing residents to seamlessly manage their smart home features from the moment they walk through the door. Experience the future of connected living, designed for the people building it.

What the HSITP is — and why its dormitories matter

HSITP sits at the centre of Hong Kong's innovation strategy: a vast research and development park developed jointly with Shenzhen, intended to anchor deep-tech, biotech, AI, and advanced manufacturing talent on Hong Kong soil. At three times the size of Hong Kong Science Park, its scale signals the seriousness of the ambition — this is not an incubator with a nice lobby, but a working district where thousands of innovation professionals will live as well as work.

That is why the dormitories matter: the researchers and founders HSITP must attract have the highest expectations of technology, and will judge a "smart" residence by the standards of the products they build. The foyer smart mirror is the first thing a resident touches every day.

For Oolaa, a company with 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau and its own factory in Shenzhen, supplying HSITP is a natural fit: a Hong Kong innovator, manufacturing across the border, equipping the very park built to celebrate that cross-border innovation story. The foyer mirror as a smart-home control hub

Why the foyer? Because it is the hinge of the home — the point every resident passes when leaving and returning. A smart mirror at the foyer becomes a household command centre with a glanceable display: check the weather before heading out, glance at the calendar, confirm the home is secured, all while putting on shoes.

The control layer is what makes it powerful. Oolaa smart mirrors support voice control through Google Assistant, with Cantonese supported — residents can speak naturally to manage connected devices. Underneath, the mirror speaks the full range of smart-home protocols: Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and KNX. Through Matter, it interoperates with the major ecosystems — Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and Xiaomi — so residents are not locked into a single brand of devices.

Picture the leaving-home routine: one voice command or tap at the foyer mirror, and the dormitory's lights switch off, the air conditioning eases back, and the door locks behind you. In the evening, the mirror greets the returning resident with lighting scenes and the day's remaining schedule. This is the "seamless management of smart home features" the project promises — not a collection of apps, but a single surface at the natural control point of the home.

A day in a smart HSITP dormitory

Morning: the mirror shows weather and MTR status while she checks her appearance. She says good morning in Cantonese; the mirror confirms the apartment is set to away mode.

Evening: she casts a video call to the mirror while preparing dinner — the same "No Login. Just Cast." simplicity, with no accounts or pairing rituals.

These are scenarios, not promises about the final HSITP fit-out — but they illustrate why developers of staff quarters, serviced apartments, and co-living projects increasingly specify a foyer control surface. The mirror earns its place not as a gadget, but as the room's most-used interface.

Specifying foyer mirrors for residential projects

If you are developing staff quarters, serviced apartments, student housing, or co-living space, the HSITP approach offers a template:

  • Put the interface where the routine is. The foyer is passed twice daily by every resident. A control surface anywhere else gets a fraction of the use.
  • Demand open protocols. Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and KNX support means the mirror works with the devices residents already own, across Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and Xiaomi ecosystems.
  • Support the local language. Cantonese voice control via Google Assistant makes the system genuinely usable for Hong Kong residents, not just English-speaking expatriates.
  • Keep onboarding at zero. Dormitory residents turn over. "No Login. Just Cast." means each new arrival is productive immediately — no accounts to create, no handover training.
  • Buy factory-direct for scale. Equipping every unit in a large development demands reliable volume supply and consistent after-sales. Oolaa's own Shenzhen factory exists for exactly this kind of project.
