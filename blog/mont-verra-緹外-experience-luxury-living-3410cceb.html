Smart Mirrors in Kerry's Mont Verra Luxury Mansions

February 04, 2025

In early 2025, Oolaa added one of the most prestigious names in Hong Kong luxury property to its project portfolio: Mont Verra, the opulent house development by Kerry Properties. Across the development, 64 state-of-the-art Oolaa Smart Mirrors were installed — one in every master bathroom. Mont Verra was reported in 2024 as carrying a valuation of US$128 million, placing it firmly among Hong Kong's most exclusive residential addresses. When a development of this calibre specifies a smart mirror as standard equipment in every master bathroom, it sends a clear signal about where luxury residential design is heading.

Mont Verra: a benchmark for high-tech luxury living

Hong Kong's top-tier developers no longer compete on marble and square footage alone. At the ultra-luxury level, buyers expect technology to be woven invisibly into the home — present when wanted, invisible when not. Kerry Properties' Mont Verra answers that expectation with a development where intelligence is part of the architecture, not an afterthought bolted on after handover.

The decision to place an Oolaa Smart Mirror in each master bathroom reflects a broader shift in luxury residential specification. The bathroom has become one of the most technology-dense rooms in a premium home: lighting scenes, demisting systems, and now an interactive mirror that doubles as an entertainment display. For a house development positioned at the very top of the market, the master bathroom is a showroom for the developer's vision — and the mirror is its centrepiece.

This project also illustrates a pattern Oolaa sees across its Hong Kong residential work: developers who have used Oolaa products across many projects keep returning, because a proven supply chain matters more than novelty when dozens of units must be delivered, installed, and supported on schedule.

Why the master bathroom gets the smart mirror

The master bathroom is where the smart mirror earns its place most convincingly. Consider a typical morning: while getting ready, the mirror can show the day's news, the weather, or a video call — all on a display that vanishes back into a flawless mirror surface the moment it is switched off. No black rectangle on the wall, no compromise to the interior design.

Three features make this work in a real bathroom environment. First, the built-in defogger keeps the mirror clear even after a hot shower, so the display and the reflection stay usable at exactly the moment they are needed most. Second, the IP65 rating means the unit is engineered for sustained exposure to moisture and steam — a meaningful step above the IPX4 rating found on many consumer-grade alternatives. Third, motion detection allows the mirror to wake as someone approaches and sleep when the room is empty, saving energy without any conscious effort from the user.

For luxury buyers, there is also a quieter benefit: a mirror TV preserves the calm, uncluttered aesthetic that high-end bathroom design demands. The technology disappears into the architecture, which is precisely what a US$128-million development cannot afford to get wrong.

The technology inside each installation

Every Oolaa Smart Mirror is built from three core parts: a mirror (or glass) surface with a touch film, an LED display panel, and a motherboard housed behind a metal back cover. This structure matters for a large development because it is serviceable — components can be maintained and supported over the life of the building, rather than treated as disposable electronics.

The flagship experience is summed up in four words: "No Login. Just Cast." Residents can cast content from their phones to the mirror without creating accounts, typing passwords on a remote, or learning a new interface. In a household with family members, domestic helpers, and guests, zero-login casting removes the single biggest friction point of smart displays.

Voice control is available through Google Assistant, with Cantonese supported — an important detail in Hong Kong, where many residents prefer to speak to their homes in their mother tongue. Combined with broad smart-home protocol support including Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and KNX, the mirror can also act as a control point for the wider connected home.

What developers can learn from the Mont Verra specification

If you are a developer, architect, or interior designer specifying technology for a premium residential project, the Mont Verra project offers a practical checklist:

  • Specify the bathroom early. A smart mirror needs power, data, and correct wall preparation. Deciding at the design stage avoids costly retrofits.
  • Demand a real waterproof rating. IP65 with a built-in defogger is the baseline for a mirror that will live above a basin in a steamy bathroom.
  • Protect the interior design. A mirror TV that reads as a pure mirror when off keeps the designer's vision intact.
  • Choose a supplier with project experience. Oolaa's 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau, including projects for major developers such as Sun Hung Kai, mean the logistics of a 64-unit installation are routine, not experimental.
  • Insist on factory-direct support. Oolaa designs and manufactures in its own Shenzhen factory, so specification questions, custom sizes, and after-sales service go straight to the source — not through a trading company.
