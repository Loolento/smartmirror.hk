HSITP Smart Mirrors for Innovation Talent Living

January 3, 2024

The Hong Kong-Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park (HSITP) spans 87.7 hectares and is being developed in two phases — and its vision of the future includes something personal: a smart mirror in every talent apartment. For the researchers and specialists the park aims to attract, home will mean controlling the smart home by voice and catching real-time information on the mirror — a more comfortable home, and a daily taste of the smart life the park exists to create.

An 87.7-Hectare Bet on Innovation

Scale first: at 87.7 hectares, developed in two phases, HSITP is one of Hong Kong’s most ambitious innovation projects. Phase one alone represents around one million square metres of gross floor area, planned to house wet laboratories and advanced-manufacturing R&D buildings, dry laboratories and offices, industry-academia-research collaboration spaces, talent apartments, visitor accommodation, and commercial and supporting facilities. Beyond the first batch of works, the remaining phase-one land is being advanced through market-driven development to accelerate high-quality research and industrial facilities.

Parks like this compete globally for one scarce resource: talent. World-class researchers can choose where to work, and the decision is never about the laboratory alone. It is about the whole life around the lab — where they will live, how comfortable it will be, and whether the environment matches the ambition of the work. That is why talent apartments matter as much as laboratories in the park’s planning, and why what goes inside those apartments — down to the bathroom mirror — is a strategic decision, not an afterthought.

Talent Apartments with Smart Mirrors in Every Unit

Here is the detail that caught our eye: every talent apartment in the park is planned to include a smart mirror, giving incoming specialists an exceptional living environment from day one. Beyond controlling the smart home, the mirror keeps residents connected to real-time information — weather, news, schedules — making the home more comfortable and bringing the pleasure of the smart home into daily routines.

Think about the arrival experience. A researcher relocates from overseas, moves into a talent apartment, and the bathroom mirror greets them with the day’s weather in a new city, the headlines, and voice control over the apartment’s smart features — in a language they can use from the first morning. No setup manuals, no configuration weekends. For someone whose working hours are precious, a home that simply works is a genuine quality-of-life benefit — and a reason to stay.

There is also a quiet symbolism in the choice. A park devoted to innovation and technology houses its talent in homes that embody innovation and technology. The medium is the message: HSITP doesn’t just research the future, it lives in it. When visiting delegations tour a talent apartment and see the mirror in action, the park’s proposition makes itself.

What a Smart Mirror Does for a Busy Researcher

The use cases write themselves around a researcher’s day. Morning: weather, traffic, and the day’s schedule on the mirror while getting ready — the fastest possible start. Evening: news and messages caught up on during the wind-down routine. Throughout: voice control — with Cantonese supported via Google Assistant — for the apartment’s smart features without breaking focus from a paper or a call.

Under the surface, the connectivity matters. Oolaa devices support the full protocol range — Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and KNX — and via Matter they interoperate with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and Xiaomi ecosystems. For talent arriving with their own devices from different ecosystems, that interoperability means the apartment’s intelligence welcomes whatever they brought with them. And with the “No Login. Just Cast.” approach, sharing content to the mirror needs no accounts and leaves no personal data behind — exactly right for accommodation that will house successive residents over the years.

Lessons for Developers: Smart Home as Standard, Not Upgrade

HSITP’s decision carries a lesson for every residential developer in Hong Kong: the market is moving from smart home as an upgrade option to smart home as a standard expectation. When a major innovation park specifies smart mirrors for every talent apartment, it normalises the idea that intelligent fittings are simply what a well-built home includes — like double glazing or a video intercom a generation ago.

Developers watching this shift should note three things. First, standardisation across units keeps costs predictable and operations simple — one product, one support relationship. Second, choosing a Hong Kong manufacturer with an own factory in Shenzhen and 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau de-risks delivery at volume. Third, the resident experience must need no manual: the HSITP vision works because a mirror is already familiar; the intelligence is a bonus, not a burden.

The smart city is often discussed in terms of infrastructure and data. HSITP reminds us it is also about mornings: a researcher, new to Hong Kong, learning the weather from the bathroom mirror before an important experiment.
