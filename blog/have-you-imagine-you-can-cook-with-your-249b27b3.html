Cook with Your Kitchen Assistant: Cantonese Voice

May 23, 2020

A few years ago, if you had told a Hong Kong home cook they would one day prepare dinner by talking to their kitchen, the answer would have been “no” — never say never. Oolaa’s tech team has spent years developing Cantonese voice command with continuous improvement, and that once-impossible dream is now an everyday reality: your kitchen assistant listens, understands, and helps you cook, completely hands-free.

Why Cantonese Voice Control Changes the Kitchen

The kitchen is the one room where your hands are almost always busy — or wet, floury, or oily. That makes it the worst place for touchscreens and the best place for voice. With Cantonese voice command, you can set a timer without touching anything, ask for the next step of a recipe while your hands are in the dough, or turn on the kitchen TV’s cooking video without smearing the screen.

The Cantonese part matters enormously. Hong Kong cooks think and speak in Cantonese, including its cooking vocabulary — the names of dishes, ingredients, and techniques that generic voice assistants simply do not understand. A voice system tuned for Cantonese, continuously improved by a local tech team, understands “steam it for ten more minutes” the way a Hong Kong cook means it. Technology that speaks your language stops being technology and starts being a helper.

There is also a hygiene dividend. In a post-pandemic world, touching fewer shared surfaces in the kitchen is simply good practice. Voice control means the whole family can operate the kitchen’s smart features without everyone pressing the same buttons — a small change with outsized comfort.

How It Works: From Voice Command to Dinner on the Table

The setup is straightforward: the kitchen smart mirror or kitchen TV acts as the kitchen’s voice-enabled hub. Speak a command in Cantonese, and the system responds — playing a recipe video step by step, setting multiple timers for different dishes, reading out ingredient quantities, or playing music while you work. Because Oolaa supports Google Assistant with Cantonese, the kitchen assistant also connects to the broader smart home: “turn on the living room air conditioning” works just as well from the chopping board.

Under the hood, this is part of a wider smart-home fabric. Oolaa devices support the full range of protocols — Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and KNX — and via Matter they work with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and Xiaomi ecosystems. So the kitchen assistant is not an island: the same voice that starts your recipe video can dim the dining room lights when dinner is ready. For a household with mixed devices bought over the years, that interoperability is what makes the system feel effortless rather than fiddly.

And because the tech team keeps refining the Cantonese command set, the assistant gets better at understanding real kitchen speech — fast, colloquial, half-sentences shouted over a sizzling wok. That continuous improvement is the difference between a demo that impresses once and a tool the family actually uses every night.

The Hong Kong Kitchen: Small Space, Big Demands

Hong Kong kitchens ask a lot of a small footprint. In a typical 300-square-foot flat, the kitchen might be barely larger than a corridor — yet it is expected to produce everything from weekday quick meals to full festive dinners. Space for another gadget simply does not exist, which is why the kitchen smart mirror earns its place: it is the cabinet door or the wall surface you already had, now with a computer behind it.

Picture the scenarios. A working parent follows a video recipe on the kitchen TV while prepping dinner in a galley kitchen, pausing and rewinding by voice with wet hands. An elderly cook who finds small phone screens hard to read gets large, clear recipe steps on the mirror at eye level. A couple hosts friends and the kitchen screen becomes the evening’s music and photo display. The common thread: the kitchen stops being a place where technology gets in the way, and becomes a place where it quietly helps.

As the market leader in kitchen TV in Hong Kong, Oolaa has watched these patterns across thousands of homes. The lesson is consistent: kitchen technology succeeds when it disappears into the cooking flow. Voice control in your own language is the most invisible interface of all — which is why it works.
