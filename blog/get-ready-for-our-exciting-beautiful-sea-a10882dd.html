A new address on the Kai Tak shoreline

Kai Tak is Hong Kong's great urban second act — the old airport lands reborn as a new district facing the harbour. A development on its seashore sells a particular promise: space, water, and light, in a city that rarely offers all three at once. Homes in a setting like that are specified to a standard, and the bathrooms are part of the specification, not an afterthought.

The setting deserves a sentence of its own. Kai Tak, where Hong Kong's airport once stood, is being rebuilt as a residential district on the harbour's edge — and a seashore address in Hong Kong is a promise about mornings: light on the water, weather worth checking, a view that starts the day before the day starts. Bathrooms in homes like these are specified as part of that promise.

Smart mirrors in the bathrooms

For NKIL6552, the bathrooms are fitted with Oolaa Smart Mirrors. It is the room where smart glass makes the most daily sense: the mirror is where residents start the day, and an Oolaa mirror meets them there with the time, the weather, and the day's information on the glass — then steps back into a clean reflection when it is time to simply get ready.

The mirrors are built for the room they live in. Oolaa's bathroom mirrors carry IP65 protection against the steam and splashes of daily use, a built-in defogger so the glass clears after a hot shower, and motion detection that wakes the display as someone approaches. This is home ground for Oolaa — Hong Kong's market leader in bathroom TV, with decades of bathroom installations behind the badge.

There is a practical side to the poetry. A bathroom mirror that shows the day's weather — the actual weather, over the actual harbour outside the window — plus the time and the morning's information, earns its place in the first five minutes of every day. Add a glass that refuses to fog and a display that wakes as you approach, and the mirror becomes the room's quiet concierge.
