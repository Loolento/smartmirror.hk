Smart Doorbell Privacy Notice at Manor Hill, HK

March 17, 2023

In a video filmed at the gate of Kane Estate (Manor Hill), our smart doorbell was mentioned at the 4:17 mark — and the host raised a fair and important question: do smart doorbells invade privacy? We welcome the question, because privacy deserves straight answers. So here is ours, clearly: the smart doorbell we supplied does not have a video recording function, and its camera does not switch on by itself. A snapshot is only ever taken when a visitor presses the bell.

What the Video Showed — and What It Didn’t

To be precise about what happened: during a walkthrough video at the Kane Estate gate, the host noticed the smart doorbell unit we installed and voiced a concern that such devices might record residents and visitors without their knowledge. It is a reasonable concern in an age of doorbell cameras that stream to the cloud — but it does not describe our product.

Our doorbell is a notification device, not a surveillance device. It has no video recording function at all: there is no continuous footage, no stored clips, and no cloud library of comings and goings. Its image capture exists for one purpose only — to show you who is at the door when they press the bell — and it does not activate automatically on its own. If nobody presses the bell, the camera stays off. That is the whole story, and we would rather state it plainly than leave any room for doubt.

Recording vs. Non-Recording Doorbells: A Key Distinction

Much of the public anxiety about “smart doorbells” comes from lumping very different products together. On one end of the spectrum are camera doorbells that record continuously, upload footage to remote servers, and use motion detection to capture everyone who walks past — postman, neighbour, or stranger. Those products raise genuine questions about consent, data storage, and who can access the footage.

On the other end are devices like ours: a smart chime with on-demand snapshot. Nothing is recorded, nothing is uploaded in the background, and there is no library of footage for anyone to browse, share, or leak. The privacy equation is completely different when there is simply no recording to worry about. When evaluating any smart doorbell, the first question to ask is not how smart it is, but what it records, where the data goes, and who controls it. If the answer is “nothing is recorded,” most privacy concerns fall away at the root.

Privacy by Design: What Estates Should Ask Before Specifying

For property developers, estate managers, and incorporated owners weighing up a smart doorbell for a gatehouse or residential entrance, we suggest four questions. First: does the device record video, or does it only capture on demand? Second: does the camera activate automatically — by motion, for example — or only when deliberately triggered? Third: where does any captured image go — to a local display, to residents’ phones, or to a third-party server? Fourth: can the estate switch features off, or is data collection baked in?

Products designed around “no recording, no automatic activation” are the simplest fit for Hong Kong residential estates, where gates and lobbies are shared spaces used by hundreds of residents daily. They deliver the convenience — knowing who is at the door, managing visitors and deliveries — without creating a footage archive that nobody asked for and everybody would rather not be responsible for. As a Hong Kong company with 31,000+ installations across the city and Macau, we have learned that the most successful estate technology is the kind residents never have to think about.

Why Estates Still Want Smart Doorbells at the Gate

Given the privacy debate, it is worth asking why estates install smart doorbells at all. The answer is everyday practicality. At a busy estate gate like Kane Estate’s, the doorbell is the first point of contact for visitors, couriers, and contractors. A smart doorbell lets the gatehouse or the resident see who is calling without walking to the gate, log visitor requests more efficiently, and handle the flood of parcel deliveries that defines modern estate life.

Consider the common scenarios: a courier arrives while the resident is upstairs; a visitor presses the bell and the resident confirms them from the living room; an elderly resident living alone can check who is at the door without opening it. None of these requires recording anyone. They require a reliable, simple device that does its job and then stays quiet — which is exactly what a non-recording smart doorbell is. Technology earns its place at the gate by being useful without being intrusive, and that is the standard we build to.
