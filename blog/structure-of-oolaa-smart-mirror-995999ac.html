Inside an Oolaa Smart Mirror: 3-Part Structure

April 15, 2024

What is actually inside a smart mirror? Strip away the marketing, and every Oolaa Smart Mirror is built from exactly three parts. Part A: the mirror (or glass) surface with a touch film. Part B: the LED display panel. Part C: the motherboard with the metal back cover of the whole unit. That is the complete anatomy — and understanding these three parts explains nearly everything about how a smart mirror looks, performs, and lasts. Whether you are a developer specifying hundreds of units or a homeowner choosing one, this is the engineering you are buying.

Part A: the mirror surface and touch film

The front of the unit is the part you see and touch every day — and it has the hardest job. It must be a genuinely good mirror first: flat, distortion-free, and reflective enough for shaving, makeup, and getting dressed. Behind that reflective surface sits the display window, an area where the mirror coating is engineered to let the LED panel's light through. When the display is off, the whole surface reads as one continuous mirror; when it is on, video and information appear within the reflection.

Laminated to this surface is the touch film — a transparent capacitive layer that turns the mirror itself into a control surface. There is no remote to lose, no buttons to collect grime, and nothing protruding from the wall. A tap or swipe on the glass is enough to change channels, adjust volume, or navigate menus. In bathrooms and kitchens, where hands are often wet, touch-through-glass is far more practical than any handheld controller.

Part A's quality separates a convincing mirror TV from a disappointing one: a cheap surface shows edge distortion, and a poor display window reveals a visible rectangle with the screen off. Oolaa's own Shenzhen factory exists largely to get this single surface right.

Part B: the LED display panel

Behind the mirror surface sits the LED panel — the actual screen. This is a purpose-selected display, not a repurposed television panel: it must be bright enough to punch through the semi-reflective mirror coating, with viewing angles wide enough for a bathroom where the viewer stands close and off-centre.

Brightness is the specification most buyers never ask about: the mirror coating absorbs light, so the panel must output far more brightness than a normal TV. Underspecify it and the picture washes out; match panel to coating — possible only when one manufacturer controls both — and video stays crisp under vanity lights.

Display-window size and placement are design decisions too: full-mirror for media-heavy use, a smaller window to preserve grooming area. Oolaa's own manufacturing tailors these to each project.

Part C: the motherboard and metal back cover

The third part is the brain and the body: the motherboard that runs the smart functions, enclosed by the metal back cover that seals the whole unit. This is where the "smart" lives — the casting receiver for "No Login. Just Cast.", the touch controller interpreting the film's signals, the voice-assistant integration, and the connectivity stack spanning Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and the smart-home protocols.

The metal back cover provides rigidity, electromagnetic shielding, and environmental sealing. In a bathroom, the unit's back faces a moist wall cavity; the sealed metal enclosure plus the IP65 rating keeps steam out for years — where plastic housings and open vents fail.

The three-part structure also has a practical consequence buyers should value: serviceability. Because the mirror is assembled from three distinct, engineered parts rather than glued into a disposable slab, individual parts can be serviced and supported. For a developer installing dozens or hundreds of units across a project with a multi-decade lifespan, that serviceability is the difference between an asset and a liability.

What this structure means for buyers

If you are evaluating smart mirrors — for a single home or a whole development — the three-part anatomy gives you the right questions to ask any supplier:

  • Who makes the mirror surface? Ask to see the display window with the screen off. If you can see the rectangle, the optical engineering is not good enough.
  • Is the panel matched to the mirror? Brightness must be specified for viewing through a reflective coating, not for open-air use.
  • What is the waterproof rating? For bathrooms, IP65 with a built-in defogger is Oolaa's standard — accept nothing vague like "moisture resistant."
  • Can it be serviced? A three-part serviceable structure beats a sealed disposable unit, especially at project scale.
  • Who stands behind it? Oolaa's 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau, backed by its own Shenzhen factory, mean specification questions and after-sales go straight to the manufacturer.
