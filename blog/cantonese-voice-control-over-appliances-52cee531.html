Cantonese Voice Control for Smart Home Appliances

May 07, 2023

If the only advantage of a smart home is that you can control your appliances with your phone, it is fair to ask an awkward question: why do you have to unlock your phone and search for an app just to turn on the lights? Operation like that makes a smart home less convenient and less practical — the opposite of what it promises. Watch the demonstration above to see the alternative.

The phone-app paradox

The phone was supposed to be the universal remote. In practice, it is a locked door: pick it up, unlock it, find the right app, wait for it to load, find the right device, then tap. Multiply that small ceremony by every light, every appliance and every evening, and the "smart" home starts to feel like extra work. Voice control removes the queue entirely — you speak, and the home responds.

The language barrier holding Hong Kong back

One of the reasons the development of the smart home market in Hong Kong has been limited is language. Control has largely meant voice commands in English or Mandarin only — which, for Cantonese-speaking households, feels less humane and less friendly. A home you cannot talk to in your own language never quite feels like yours; technology that asks you to switch languages to switch on a light has the relationship backwards.

Today, appliances answer in Cantonese

That barrier is now gone: we finally have a way to control appliances in Cantonese. With Oolaa, voice control runs through Google Assistant with Cantonese support, so the home is operated the natural way — you speak in Cantonese, and your appliances respond. No unlocking, no app-hunting, no performing a second language for your own living room.

The hope behind it is the one this project started with: that controlling appliances in Cantonese can benefit the people of Hong Kong — and promote the development of the local smart home market into what it always should have been.
