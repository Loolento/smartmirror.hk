Oolaa Smart Mirror in Manor Hill Clubhouse

March 17, 2023

Oolaa supplied its cutting-edge smart mirror to the luxurious Manor Hill clubhouse in Tseung Kwan O, Hong Kong. The installation is an innovative crossover with KaraMirror: the Oolaa Smart Mirror hardware becomes a platform for KaraMirror's comprehensive fitness programming, giving residents guided workouts in their own clubhouse. It is a telling example of how residential clubhouses in Hong Kong are evolving — from rooms full of equipment into experience-led wellness destinations.

The clubhouse as a wellness destination

In Hong Kong's residential market, the clubhouse sells the development. Buyers tour the gym, the yoga room, and the function spaces before they ever discuss floor plans, and developers invest heavily to make those spaces memorable. But traditional clubhouse gyms have a weakness: rows of machines are interchangeable. Every development has them, and none of them gives residents a reason to choose one clubhouse over another.

The Manor Hill installation answers that problem with differentiation through experience. A smart mirror running guided fitness content is not another machine — it is an attraction. Residents do not just exercise; they follow programmes, track progress, and engage with content that changes. For the developer, that translates into a clubhouse story worth telling in the sales brochure. For the property manager, it means a facility residents actually use rather than photograph once and forget.

This project also reflects Oolaa's breadth across Hong Kong residential work. With 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau — spanning bathrooms, kitchens, lobbies, and now clubhouse fitness spaces — the company has become the market leader in bringing display technology into the rooms where developers need differentiation most. KaraMirror: fitness content meets mirror hardware

The crossover concept is straightforward but powerful. Oolaa provides the smart mirror — a full-size mirror with an integrated display, touch interaction, and the build quality refined across years of manufacturing. KaraMirror provides the fitness programming: structured, guided workout content designed for all residents, from beginners to the already-active.

The mirror form factor is ideal for fitness. Unlike a television mounted across the room, a mirror lets users watch their own form while following the instructor — the single most important feedback loop in any workout. The display overlays guidance onto the reflection, so residents can correct posture in real time instead of discovering bad habits weeks later. For group class settings in a clubhouse studio, one large mirror display can lead a whole room.

Because the unit is a genuine mirror when the display is off, it also solves the aesthetic problem that plagues clubhouse tech upgrades. A studio lined with mirrors looks like a premium fitness space whether the system is running or not — no black screens deadening the room between sessions.

What residents actually get

Consider the resident's experience. Instead of booking an external gym membership or following phone-screen workouts at home, Manor Hill residents can walk downstairs to a guided fitness programme in their own building. The barrier to exercise collapses: no commute, no monthly fee, no waiting for equipment.

Comprehensive programming matters here. A single workout video gets stale; a programme with progression — beginner to intermediate, strength to mobility — keeps residents returning across months. That sustained engagement is what turns a clubhouse from a sales feature into a lived amenity, and it is what protects the developer's investment long after the last unit is sold.

For families, the appeal widens further. A mirror-based fitness system is approachable for teenagers and older residents alike — there is no intimidating weight room culture, just a mirror that guides. In a multigenerational residential community, that accessibility is a genuine differentiator.

A specification guide for clubhouse operators

If you operate or are fitting out a residential clubhouse, hotel gym, or corporate wellness space, the Manor Hill project suggests a practical checklist:

  • Choose experience over equipment. Interactive, content-driven installations differentiate a clubhouse; another treadmill does not.
  • Insist on the mirror form factor. Real-time form feedback is the core training benefit, and a true mirror surface preserves the studio aesthetic when the display is off.
  • Demand content with progression. Comprehensive programming — not a handful of videos — is what sustains resident engagement over time.
  • Plan for shared use. Clubhouse installations serve many residents daily. Commercial-grade build quality and a supplier with local after-sales support are non-negotiable.
  • Work factory-direct. Custom sizes for studio walls and integration with the interior design are only practical when the supplier controls manufacturing, as Oolaa does from its Shenzhen factory.
