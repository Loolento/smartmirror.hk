Eminence Terrace: Oolaa Mirror in Hung Shui Kiu

January 29, 2026

As Hong Kong advances toward its smart-city vision, smart living is becoming key to quality of life — and it is reaching everyone. Oolaa’s first public housing project brings the smart mirror to the lift lobby of Eminence Terrace I in Hung Shui Kiu: real-time information for residents, a new communication bridge for the community, and a modern lift lobby with genuine presence. This is smart-city technology where people actually live.

Oolaa’s First Public Housing Project — and Why It Matters

Until now, you might have associated smart mirrors with luxury developments and five-star hotels. Eminence Terrace I changes that picture: it is Oolaa’s first public housing project, and it proves that smart-living technology is not a luxury extra but a community utility. When technology serves hundreds of households a day in a lift lobby, its value is measured in everyday convenience multiplied across a whole estate.

The significance goes beyond one building. Hong Kong’s smart-city blueprint talks about using innovation and technology to improve daily life — and daily life happens in lift lobbies, mailrooms, and estate walkways. Putting an intelligent, always-on information surface in the lift lobby is smart-city policy made tangible: residents don’t read about the smart city, they use it while waiting for the lift.

The Lift Lobby: The Most Underused Screen in the Building

Think about the lift lobby’s unique position. Every resident passes through it at least twice a day; it is the one place in the building with a truly captive, recurring audience. Yet in most estates it carries nothing but a notice board that few people stop to read. A smart mirror transforms this dead space into the building’s information hub — without taking up any more room than a mirror would.

The design upgrade matters too. A large, elegant smart mirror gives the lift lobby a modern, cared-for feel the moment residents and visitors step in. First impressions shape how people feel about where they live; a lobby that looks considered tells residents their home is considered. Technology here does double duty: it informs, and it dignifies the shared space.

Real-Time Information, Zero Distance

So what does the lift-lobby smart mirror actually do for residents? Start with the morning: real-time weather and traffic information while you wait for the lift, so you know before you leave whether to grab the umbrella or allow extra minutes for the commute. Small information, delivered at exactly the right moment, repeated across hundreds of households every day.

Then the community layer: instant building notices and property-management information, published to the lobby screen the moment they matter — maintenance schedules, estate activities, management office announcements. Compared with paper notices that fade on a board, a live screen is always current and impossible to miss for anyone passing through. Communication between management and residents goes from “hope they saw it” to “they saw it.”

Underneath it all is a simple idea: the smart mirror is not just a display, but a bridge. It connects the resident to the city (weather, traffic), to the estate (notices, management), and to a sense of modernity about the place they call home. That is what “well-being” looks like in practice — not an abstract goal, but information and care, delivered daily.

What Smart Living Means for Public Housing

Eminence Terrace I points to a wider opportunity. Public housing estates house a huge share of Hong Kong’s population; bringing smart-living infrastructure to their shared spaces multiplies the benefit further than any single luxury project could. Lift lobbies, clubhouses, and estate entrances are all candidates for the same treatment: useful information, beautifully presented, maintained as a service.

For housing organisations and estate managers, the lift-lobby smart mirror is also an operational asset. Notices go up instantly across all lobbies, seasonal information (typhoon arrangements, festive greetings) is a content update rather than a printing job, and the estate’s communication finally matches the speed of residents’ lives. With Oolaa’s 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau and 20+ years of manufacturing in its own Shenzhen factory, the hardware and the support are proven at scale — which is exactly what public housing deployment demands.

Smart cities are not built from flagship towers alone. They are built from thousands of small, daily interactions — a resident checking the traffic while waiting for the lift, a notice read instead of missed, a lobby that feels modern. Frequently asked questions

What is Eminence Terrace I?

Eminence Terrace I is a public housing development in Hung Shui Kiu, Hong Kong — and the site of Oolaa’s first public housing smart-mirror project, with installations in the lift lobbies.

What information does the lift-lobby mirror show?

Real-time weather and traffic for the journey ahead, plus building notices and property-management information — maintenance schedules, estate announcements, and community updates — published instantly to the screen.

Why put smart mirrors in a lift lobby rather than in flats?

The lift lobby is the one space every resident passes through daily, making it the building’s most effective communication point. One screen there serves hundreds of households — the highest impact per installation in the estate.

How does this relate to Hong Kong’s smart-city goals?

Hong Kong’s smart-city vision aims to use technology to improve daily quality of life. A lift-lobby information screen is that vision made tangible: useful, real-time city and estate information, available to every resident as part of ordinary life.
