Oolaa Ai-Play Mirror Debuts at HSITP Campus

March 22, 2025

Oolaa's Ai-Play Mirror has unveiled its first project — at Buildings 8 and 9 of the Hong Kong-Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park (HSITP). This innovative initiative combines fitness and play, offering members a fresh approach to health and wellbeing. It is the first outing for a product line that turns the mirror from an information surface into an interactive fitness companion.

What is an Ai-Play Mirror?

The concept is simple and powerful: take the full-length mirror, add an interactive display and AI, and turn exercise into play. Instead of staring at a blank wall while you stretch, the Ai-Play Mirror guides, counts, corrects, and entertains. Workouts become games; form feedback arrives in real time; progress is tracked session by session. For people who find conventional exercise boring — which is most people — the playful layer is the difference between a routine that lasts and one abandoned by February.

The "play" element matters more than it sounds. Fitness research consistently shows that adherence, not intensity, determines long-term results. A mirror that makes a ten-minute stretch session feel like a game will beat a punishing programme that nobody repeats. The Ai-Play Mirror is designed around that human truth: health comes from habits, and habits come from enjoyment.

As one of Oolaa's eight product lines — alongside the Smart Mirror, Bath/Mirror TV, Kitchen/Smart TV, Outdoor TV, Axis, Smart Bath Panel, and Ai School Kiosk — the Ai-Play Mirror extends the company's mirror expertise from the bathroom into the fitness and wellness space. The same factory-direct engineering that built 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau now serves a new room: the gym, the studio, and the wellness corner.

Why an innovation park is the perfect first stage

Buildings 8 and 9 of the HSITP house the people building Hong Kong's innovation future — researchers, engineers, and founders who spend long hours at desks and screens. It is a population that understands, better than most, the cost of a sedentary workday: stiff shoulders, tired eyes, declining energy. A fitness installation here is not decoration; it is infrastructure for the workforce.

There is also a symbolic fit. An innovation and technology park is exactly where a new category of product should debut — among members who appreciate what the technology is doing and can give the sharp feedback that improves it. First projects are learning projects, and there is no better classroom than a community of technologists.

The initiative's framing — "a fresh approach to health for all members" — points to the inclusive ambition. This is not elite athletic training; it is everyday movement made accessible and enjoyable. A ten-minute guided stretch between meetings, a playful balance challenge with colleagues, a moment of active recovery in a demanding day. Small doses, high frequency, zero intimidation.

The workplace wellness opportunity

Step back from the specific installation and a larger trend comes into focus: workplace wellness has moved from perk to strategy. Employers compete for talent partly on the quality of the workday experience, and movement — real, enjoyable movement, not a dusty treadmill in a basement — is central to it. The companies winning the talent war are the ones whose offices feel alive.

An interactive fitness mirror fits this trend unusually well. Unlike a full gym, it needs only wall space. Unlike fitness classes, it needs no scheduling and no instructor. Unlike app-based programmes, it is physically present — a visible invitation in the corridor that normalises taking a movement break. For facility managers, the equation is attractive: high visibility, low footprint, no staffing, and usage data that demonstrates the investment is working.

Consider a typical scenario. It is 3pm, energy is dipping, and a team has been in meetings since morning. Two colleagues stop at the Ai-Play Mirror for a five-minute guided stretch and a light coordination game. They return to their desks looser, more alert, and — not incidentally — having shared a small moment of fun. Multiply that across a building, across a year, and the cultural effect is real. Wellness stops being a poster in the lift and becomes something people actually do.

What facility managers should look for

If you operate a corporate campus, clubhouse, residential tower, or wellness facility and are evaluating interactive fitness mirrors, four criteria matter. First, the content: does the mirror offer guided, progressive programming — or just a screen with videos? Guidance and feedback are what separate a fitness product from a television. Second, the play factor: will a beginner enjoy the first session enough to come back for a second? Third, durability and support: commercial environments are harsh, and the supplier should be a manufacturer with a service history, not a gadget importer. Fourth, data: can the facility see usage and demonstrate value?

Oolaa's position on the third criterion is unusually strong. As a Hong Kong company with its own factory in Shenzhen and 20+ years in business, it builds what it sells and services what it builds — the same capability behind its market-leading bathroom TV and kitchen TV lines. For a facility manager signing off on a multi-year installation, that continuity matters more than any single feature.

The HSITP debut is the beginning of the story, not the end. As the Ai-Play Mirror moves from innovation parks into clubhouses, offices, schools, and homes, the core promise stays the same: fitness that feels like play, in a mirror that was already part of the room.
