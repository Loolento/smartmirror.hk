Smart Mirror Uses: Hallway, Bathroom and Beyond

Aug 12, 2022

Installing a smart mirror in the hallway makes your home feel completely different. Oolaa offers plenty of sizes and series for different areas of the home — and each location unlocks a different kind of value. Not sure which one might suit you? This guide walks through the applications room by room, so you can match the mirror to the moment.

The hallway and foyer: your home's command centre

The hallway mirror is the most underrated smart home upgrade there is. It is the last thing you see before leaving and the first thing that greets you on return — which makes it the natural place for the information that frames your day: time, weather, calendar reminders, traffic. A smart mirror here turns a passive glance into an informed departure.

But the foyer mirror can be more than a display. Oolaa's foyer mirror is designed as a whole-home control hub, issuing commands via Google Assistant, Bluetooth and Zigbee. Standing at the door, you can check that the lights are off, adjust the air conditioning, or speak a Cantonese voice command to shut down the home as you leave. For a busy household, that single point of control at the natural exit point of the home is genuinely transformative — which is exactly why the hallway is where many smart mirror journeys begin.

The bathroom: the hardest-working mirror in the house

The bathroom mirror gets more face time than any other mirror in the home — morning and night, every day. A smart mirror here earns its place through the daily routine: time and weather while you brush your teeth, news headlines while you get ready, music or video during a long bath. Oolaa's bathroom mirrors are engineered for this environment, built to IP65 — a higher moisture-protection rating than the IPX4 common in the category — with a built-in defogger and motion detection, so steam never interrupts the experience.

For those who want more than information, the mirror TV takes the bathroom mirror further: a special mirror coating turns the surface into a television when switched on, invisible as a mirror when off. Oolaa is the market leader in Hong Kong bathroom TV — the same technology installed at Park Hyatt Niseko and the Waldorf Astoria Bangkok — bringing five-star bathroom entertainment into the home.

The kitchen: information where your hands are busy

The kitchen is where a smart display makes the most practical sense: your hands are wet, covered in flour, or holding a knife — the worst possible moment to touch a phone. A smart mirror or kitchen TV on the wall lets you follow recipes, watch the news, or video-call family while you cook, all without touching a thing. Voice control completes the picture: speak a command in Cantonese and the kitchen responds.

Oolaa is the market leader in Hong Kong kitchen TV as well as bathroom TV, with products designed for the heat, steam and splashes of a working kitchen. A kitchen screen is one of those upgrades that sounds like a luxury until you have cooked with one — after which going back feels like losing a sense organ.

Bedrooms, living rooms and commercial spaces

Beyond the big three, smart mirrors find their place wherever people pause and look. A bedroom vanity mirror with backlighting and information display streamlines the morning routine. A living room mirror TV preserves the room's design — no black rectangle dominating the wall — while adding entertainment on demand. And commercially, the applications multiply: hotel bathrooms and lobbies, serviced apartments, clubhouses, gyms and salons all use smart mirrors to combine function with impression.

With 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau spanning residential, hotel and commercial projects, Oolaa has seen the smart mirror work in just about every setting imaginable. The pattern is consistent: wherever a mirror already belongs, a smart mirror belongs more.

How to choose the right size and series

With plenty of sizes and series available, choosing comes down to three questions. First: where will it live? Bathrooms need IP65-rated, defogger-equipped models; hallways benefit from hub-capable foyer mirrors; kitchens need heat- and splash-resistant screens. Second: what is the viewing distance? A vanity mirror is viewed from arm's length, so display text must be legible up close; a living room mirror TV is viewed from metres away, so screen size matters more. Third: how will it connect? Confirm compatibility with your smart-home platforms — Oolaa mirrors work with iOS and Google smart home, and the smart-home range supports Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and KNX, connecting with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa and Xiaomi.
