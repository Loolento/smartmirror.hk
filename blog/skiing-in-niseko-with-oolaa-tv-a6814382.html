Park Hyatt Niseko Bathrooms Feature Oolaa TV

February 3, 2025

Even while enjoying the skiing in Niseko, Japan, guests can indulge in the luxury of an Oolaa Mirror TV — a screen designed to deliver exceptional entertainment without disturbing the calm of a mountain hotel bathroom. The prestigious Park Hyatt Niseko features an Oolaa Mirror TV in every bathroom, so guests enjoy smart entertainment and innovative technology throughout their stay.

Niseko is a particular kind of destination. Guests spend their days on some of the most celebrated powder snow on earth and their evenings recovering from it. The bathroom of a ski hotel is not a transit space; it is part of the ritual — the long soak, the slow thaw. A television that belongs there has to respect the room: present when wanted, invisible when not. The Mirror TV's display area is finished as a mirror, so the glass reads as a mirror first and a screen second.

A Mirror TV in every bathroom

Consistency is the Park Hyatt standard, and it is harder than it sounds. One beautiful bathroom is a photograph; a television in every bathroom is an operation — the same experience, room after room, floor after floor, for every guest who checks in. Specifying the same Oolaa Mirror TV across the property gives the hotel exactly that: entertainment in the bathroom that works the same way everywhere, backed by a single manufacturer rather than a patchwork of local purchases.

For the guest, the appeal is immediate. Watch the news with the morning routine. Keep a film running through the evening soak. The bathroom stops being the one room in the suite where the experience pauses.

The B-CAS detail most visitors never hear about

Here is the part of the project that separates a manufacturer from a reseller. To access digital TV in Japan, a B-CAS card is required — something even some locals may not know. A television shipped to Japan without one is, for broadcast purposes, furniture. Any supplier can sell a hotel a screen; making that screen actually receive Japanese digital television, in every room, is a different order of work.

Oolaa handled it at the source. As a leading manufacturer, Oolaa produced 277 state-of-the-art Mirror TVs equipped with B-CAS cards in China — in its own factory — offering the hotel a cost-saving solution without compromising on quality or service excellence. The cards were not an afterthought or a local workaround; they were part of the production specification, unit after unit.

What this project shows about hotel bathroom TV

Three lessons travel well beyond Hokkaido. First, local broadcast standards are a specification, not a detail — check them before the order, not after the installation. Second, factory-direct production changes what is possible: a requirement like B-CAS, applied to 277 units, is simple when the specifier and the manufacturer are the same company. Third, the bathroom is now a standard front in hotel competition, and the properties winning it treat the mirror as media, not plumbing.

Oolaa brings that combination — Hong Kong company, own factory in Shenzhen, 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau — to hotel and residential projects well beyond its home market. There is a fitting symmetry in it: a technology perfected for the bathrooms of Hong Kong's towers, proven again in the bathrooms of a Japanese ski resort, where the expectations of the world's most demanding hospitality market apply. Niseko's snow is a long way from Hong Kong. The standard of bathroom is not.

Specifying bathroom TVs for an international hotel

For hotel groups planning similar work, the Niseko project offers a practical sequence. Begin with the guest, not the hardware: decide what the bathroom screen is for — news and weather at dawn, entertainment through the evening soak — and let that set the size and placement. Then audit the destination's broadcast requirements before anything is ordered; Japan's B-CAS system is one example of a national standard that a generic international shipment would quietly fail. Finally, weigh the manufacturer, not just the unit price: a factory-direct producer can build destination-specific requirements into the production run itself, keep quality consistent across hundreds of rooms, and support the property long after opening day. The cheapest screen is rarely the cheapest installation.
