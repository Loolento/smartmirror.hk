Oolaa Smart Mirrors at Eight Star Street, Hong Kong

Mar 4, 2024

Eight Star Street by Swire is one of Hong Kong's most considered new addresses — and its homes are fitted with Oolaa smart mirrors. What makes the mirror worth its place on the wall is not any single trick. It is that one surface quietly does the work of a control panel, an assistant, and a security screen, then goes back to being a mirror.

The control center of the home

First, an Oolaa smart mirror can serve as the control center of the home automation system. From the mirror's touch screen, residents control the lighting, the curtains, the air conditioning, and the sound system in the room — no hunting for a wall switch, a remote, or the right app on a phone. Everything in the room answers from the glass in front of you.

The effect is practical as much as it is elegant. One consistent surface for the whole environment raises the comfort and convenience of the entire living space, because control stops being a chore distributed across a dozen devices and becomes a single, obvious gesture.

A smart assistant on the glass

Second, the mirror carries smart assistant features, in the family of voice assistants like Siri, Google Assistant, and Alexa. Residents interact with the mirror by voice: ask for the weather forecast, play music, set reminders, and more. Through voice recognition and its internet connection, the mirror provides real-time information and services — the morning's facts delivered while the morning is actually happening, hands free, in front of the glass.

In the Oolaa ecosystem, voice runs through Google Assistant with Cantonese supported — no small thing in a Hong Kong home, where the natural way to ask is the way people speak to family.

The assistant and the control center are really one idea at two speeds. The control center is for the deliberate moment — lights down, curtains drawn, the room set for the evening. The assistant is for the moment in between, when hands are busy and a spoken question is easier than a screen: the weather while dressing, a reminder set while leaving, music started without putting anything down. A home that answers at both speeds feels less like a system and more like a service.

Security, visible at a glance

Third, the mirror brings smart security into the room. Connected to the home monitoring system, it lets residents view surveillance video right on the mirror's screen, and receive instant alert notifications when there is suspicious activity or a visitor at the door. Security stops being something checked on a phone in another room; it is present, calmly, on a surface that is already part of the home.

Connected to the home network

These features compound because the mirror connects to the home network, enabling more smart operations and services over time. Oolaa smart mirrors support Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and KNX, and through Matter they work with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa and Xiaomi — so the mirror at Eight Star Street is not an island. It is the visible surface of the whole connected home.

Behind it stands Oolaa (Viewdex Limited) — Hong Kong company, own factory in Shenzhen, 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau. Eight Star Street is exactly the kind of address that specifies technology this way: invisible when you don't need it, indispensable when you do.

For buyers comparing smart-home specifications, the Eight Star Street pattern is the one to look for: a single surface that unifies control, assistance, and security, connected to the home network so it can grow with the household's devices. That is the difference between a gadget fitted to a home and infrastructure built into it.
