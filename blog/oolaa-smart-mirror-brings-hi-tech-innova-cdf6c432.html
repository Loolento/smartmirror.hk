Royal Garden Hotel Adds Oolaa Mirror TV

March 17, 2025

Oolaa has installed its state-of-the-art Mirror TV at the Royal Garden Hotel in Hong Kong. The installation brings high-tech innovation into the hotel's luxury bathrooms — merging entertainment with style, and elevating the smart living experience for guests in one of the city's premier luxury hotels. In hospitality, where every detail is judged and remembered, the bathroom mirror has become an unexpected stage for differentiation.

The hotel bathroom as a differentiator

Ask frequent travellers what they remember about a hotel room, and the bathroom ranks near the top — alongside the bed and the view. It is also the room where hotels struggle most to differentiate: stone, brassware, and lighting schemes converge toward a familiar luxury template across competing properties. Technology is one of the few remaining ways to surprise a guest in that space.

A mirror TV does exactly that. The guest walks into the bathroom and sees a flawless mirror. Then the mirror comes alive — morning news while shaving, a film during a long soak, hotel information and restaurant menus at a glance. The moment of discovery is the point: it is delightful precisely because it is unexpected, and delight is what gets mentioned in reviews and remembered on the next booking decision.

For a premier luxury hotel like the Royal Garden, the calculation is straightforward. Room rates at the top of the market must be justified by experiences guests cannot get elsewhere, and a bathroom that entertains is an experience most competitors do not offer. The mirror TV earns its place the same way a great lobby or a celebrated restaurant does — by giving guests a story to tell.

What guests actually see: the mirror TV up close

The technology is a true crossover: a high-quality mirror surface with an LED display panel sealed behind it. When the display is off, the unit is simply a mirror — no bezel-heavy television intruding on the bathroom's design. When it is on, video and information appear to float within the reflection itself.

Several engineering details matter specifically for hotel bathrooms. The built-in defogger keeps the mirror clear after hot showers, so both the reflection and the display stay usable at the moment guests need them most. The IP65 waterproof rating means the unit is built for sustained steam and moisture — well above the protection level of consumer displays that were never designed for bathrooms. Motion detection wakes the display as a guest approaches and puts it to sleep when the room empties, so the technology feels responsive without any instruction needed.

Content for hotel use typically blends entertainment with property information: television and streaming for relaxation, plus hotel messaging — spa menus, restaurant hours, checkout information — delivered where the guest is guaranteed to look. Because Oolaa's "No Login. Just Cast." approach lets guests cast from their own phones without accounts or passwords, there is no front-desk tutorial and no remote control to sanitise between stays.

Why hotels choose mirror TVs: the operator's view

From the operator's side, the mirror TV solves several practical problems at once. Space: bathrooms rarely have a wall to spare for a conventional television, but every bathroom already has a mirror — the display simply shares that surface. Hygiene: a sealed glass surface wipes down like any other bathroom fixture, with no vents or crevices collecting moisture. Durability: commercial-grade construction with a metal back cover and proper waterproofing is built for the duty cycle of a hotel room, not a living room.

There is also a quiet operational advantage in standardisation. Oolaa manufactures in its own Shenzhen factory, so a hotel group can specify identical units across properties and room categories, with consistent spares and a single after-sales relationship. For engineering departments managing hundreds of rooms, that uniformity matters more than any single feature.

And the guest-facing payoff compounds over time. A mirror TV is the kind of detail that photographs well, features in influencer content, and becomes part of a property's identity — "the hotel with the TV in the bathroom mirror." In a city as competitive as Hong Kong, that identity has real revenue value.

Specifying mirror TVs for hospitality projects

For hotel developers, operators, and hospitality designers considering bathroom displays, the Royal Garden installation suggests a checklist:

  • Protect the bathroom aesthetic. The display must read as a pure mirror when off — luxury bathroom design cannot accommodate a black rectangle.
  • Demand genuine waterproofing. IP65 with a built-in defogger is the baseline for a unit that will face steam daily for years.
  • Eliminate guest friction. Zero-login casting means no instructions, no remotes, and no front-desk support burden.
  • Plan content, not just hardware. Decide the mix of entertainment and hotel information up front — the mirror is a communication channel as well as a screen.
  • Standardise across the property. Factory-direct supply makes identical specification across room types and properties practical, with one after-sales relationship.

The luxury hotel bathroom is no longer just a beautiful room — it is a stage. The Royal Garden Hotel's mirror TV shows how a single surface can carry both the design and the delight.
