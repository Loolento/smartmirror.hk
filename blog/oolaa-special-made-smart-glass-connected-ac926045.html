Oolaa Custom Smart Glass with External Board

Apr 15, 2024

Not every project fits a standard-size mirror. Oolaa's special-made smart glass is built for those projects — a custom smart mirror divided into three engineered parts, with the motherboard moved outside the mirror body for maximum flexibility.

The three-part construction

This special-made Oolaa smart mirror is divided into three parts. Part A is the mirror (or glass) surface with touch film — the face the user sees and touches. Part B is the LED panel with the metal back cover of the whole smart mirror — the display engine, armored. Part C is an external motherboard with its own metal back cover — the computing heart, housed separately.

Each part has a job, and each part can be serviced or specified on its own terms. That separation is the entire point: a custom build should not force compromises between the visible surface, the display and the electronics.

In practice, this means the design team can finalize the mirror's look — size, shape, glass finish — while the engineering team configures the display and computing in parallel. Neither side waits on the other, and neither side's constraints leak into the other's work. For fast-moving commercial projects, that parallel workflow can save weeks.

Why the motherboard sits outside

Moving the motherboard outside the mirror body solves three problems that plague large or unusual smart mirror installations. First, heat: display panels generate warmth, and electronics prefer to live elsewhere — separation extends component life. Second, serviceability: when the computer is external, technicians can access, upgrade or replace it without disturbing a mirror that may be built into cabinetry, stone or a hotel wall. Third, size freedom: the mirror surface can be as large or as unusually shaped as the design demands, because the electronics no longer have to fit behind it.

For developers and hotel operators, that last point is the decisive one. A statement mirror in a lobby, a full-wall mirror in a penthouse bathroom, an oddly proportioned mirror dictated by architecture — all become possible when the computing does not constrain the glass.

Built like equipment, finished like furniture

Notice the metal back covers on both the mirror body and the external motherboard. This is equipment-grade construction: metal shielding protects the electronics, manages heat and gives installers something solid to mount. A smart mirror in a commercial project is infrastructure — it will be installed by contractors, lived with for a decade and serviced by people who did not design it. Metal back covers are what make that lifecycle practical.

Yet the visible side remains pure mirror or glass with touch film — no vents, no seams, no visual compromise. The engineering hides; the design shows. That duality is what separates a genuinely custom product from a standard mirror with a screen glued behind it.

When to specify special-made smart glass

Standard smart mirrors cover most residential needs. Special-made construction earns its place when the project does: oversized lobby installations, mirrors integrated into bespoke joinery, hospitality projects with brand-specific dimensions, or developments where the design team — not the product catalog — dictates the shape.

Because Oolaa is factory-direct with its own Shenzhen factory and in-house software engineering, custom builds are a manufacturing conversation, not a reseller negotiation. Sizes, glass types, touch configurations and computing can be discussed directly with the people who build them. For architects and developers, that direct line is the difference between 'the catalog does not offer that' and 'let us engineer it.'
