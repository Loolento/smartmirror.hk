Kitchen TV for Tiny Hong Kong Apartments

August 12, 2022

Don't you think it is impossible to fit a kitchen TV or smart mirror into a tiny nano apartment in Hong Kong? Most people assume the kitchen is the one room where a screen simply cannot go — there is no counter space to spare, no wall free of cabinets, and steam and grease would ruin any ordinary display within months. Oolaa is here to prove otherwise. With its factory in Shenzhen and an engineering team that designs specifically for Hong Kong's housing reality, Oolaa has developed kitchen panel solutions that bring a display into even the smallest kitchens — mounted flush, sealed against the cooking environment, and useful from the first day.

The Hong Kong nano-apartment kitchen problem

Hong Kong's new residential units keep getting smaller, and the kitchen shrinks fastest of all. In many nano apartments, the "kitchen" is a single run of cabinetry along one wall — sometimes under 2 metres wide — with every centimetre of worktop claimed by the sink, the hob, and the rice cooker. A freestanding screen has nowhere to stand, and a wall-mounted TV would steal the only splashback available.

This is the standard condition of urban Hong Kong living — and why imported smart-home products so often fail here: they are designed for spacious suburban kitchens. Oolaa's engineering team designs around the constraints of real Hong Kong floor plans, home to 31,000+ of its installations.

The kitchen panel concept solves the space problem at its root. Instead of adding a screen to the kitchen, the screen becomes part of the kitchen's surfaces — integrated into a panel that would be there anyway. When the display is off, it reads as an ordinary kitchen surface; when it is on, it is a full display for recipes, video, and information.

What a kitchen panel smart mirror actually is

At its core, the kitchen panel is a variation of Oolaa's mirror TV technology: a display panel sealed behind a glass or mirror surface. In the kitchen, the surface can be finished to match the cabinetry or splashback, so the technology disappears into the joinery. Touch control through the surface means no remote to lose and no buttons to clean — a tap or swipe on the panel itself is enough.

The engineering priorities for a kitchen are different from those for a bathroom. In a bathroom, the enemy is steam; in a kitchen, it is grease, heat, and splatter. A kitchen panel must be sealed so that cooking residue cannot work its way into the electronics, and its surface must wipe clean like any other kitchen material. Because Oolaa manufactures in its own Shenzhen factory rather than buying off-the-shelf units, these environmental requirements are designed in from the start rather than retrofitted afterwards.

Casting makes the kitchen panel genuinely practical: Oolaa's "No Login. Just Cast." lets anyone send a recipe video or the morning news from phone to display — no logins, no pairing, no login screens with flour-covered hands.

Everyday scenarios: what you actually do with it

The most natural use is also the simplest: follow a recipe video while cooking. Prop a phone against the rice cooker and the screen is too small, too far, and one splash away from disaster. A panel display at eye level, showing the same video cast from that phone, keeps the instructions visible and the phone safely in a pocket.

Morning routines are the second big use case. Hong Kong mornings are compressed — breakfast, news, weather, traffic — and much of it happens standing in the kitchen. A panel display showing the morning news while coffee brews turns dead time into informed time, without anyone needing to sit in front of a television.

For families, the panel becomes a communication surface — video calls with grandparents while cooking — and when off, it looks like an ordinary kitchen surface, costing the small kitchen none of its clean appearance.

Planning a kitchen display: a short buyer's guide

If you are a homeowner, interior designer, or developer considering a display for a compact Hong Kong kitchen, keep four points in mind:

  • Plan it with the cabinetry. A panel display works best when it is coordinated with the kitchen joinery — power supply, mounting, and surface finish should be decided together with the cabinet maker, not added after installation.
  • Demand a sealed, wipeable surface. Kitchens produce grease and steam. The display surface must clean like a splashback and keep residue out of the electronics.
  • Insist on zero-friction casting. If using the screen requires logins, apps, or pairing rituals, it will not get used with wet hands. "No Login. Just Cast." exists precisely for this reason.
  • Buy from the factory, not a reseller. Custom sizes and finishes for unusual kitchen layouts are only practical when the supplier controls manufacturing. Oolaa's own Shenzhen factory makes made-to-measure panels feasible for real projects.
