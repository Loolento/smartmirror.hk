Kitchen TV for Tiny Hong Kong Apartments

August 12, 2022

Think it is impossible to fit a kitchen TV or a smart mirror into a tiny Hong Kong nano apartment? You are not alone — most kitchen screens are designed for kitchens far bigger than the ones Hong Kong families actually cook in. Oolaa is here to help, with a smart mirror panel made for the kitchen you have, not the kitchen in a showroom.

Small kitchen, full-size information

A nano apartment's kitchen has no counter space to spare and no wall to waste, which is exactly why a flat panel is the right form. Mounted as part of the kitchen, a smart mirror panel takes no workspace at all, yet it puts everything a cook reaches for on one surface: a recipe video while both hands are in the dough, a timer that is always visible, the news or a show while dinner simmers. The cook stops propping a phone against the oil bottle — the screen is already there, at eye level, exactly where the cooking happens.

Developed for Hong Kong homes

Oolaa's factory is based in Shenzhen, and our engineering team keeps developing products that are totally suited to the housing situation in Hong Kong. That last point is the whole story. Hong Kong's nano flats and compact kitchens are not an edge case for us; they are the brief. A kitchen panel that works here has to be sized for tight walls, finished for a room that steams and splashes, and simple enough to use mid-recipe with flour on your hands. Oolaa is the market leader in kitchen TVs in Hong Kong, and this panel exists because local kitchens demanded it.

Before you add a screen to a small kitchen

Three checks before you commit. Measure the wall or cabinet face you actually have, and be honest about it — in a nano kitchen, a few centimeters decide whether the panel fits the room or fights it. Think about height: the screen should sit at eye level for the cook who will use it most, not at a standard height copied from a living room TV. And consider the finish: in a small space, a mirror surface earns its keep twice, working as a mirror and adding light to the room when the screen is off. Get those three right, and even the tiniest Hong Kong kitchen cooks like a big one.
