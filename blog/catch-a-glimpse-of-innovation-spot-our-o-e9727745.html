Oolaa Smart Mirror Spotted at HSITP Building 11

March 17, 2025

Catch a glimpse of innovation: spot our Oolaa Smart Mirror at Building 11 of the Hong Kong-Shenzhen Innovation and Technology Park (HSITP). In a park built to house the future, our mirror is on show — doing what it does best: greeting visitors with useful information, demonstrating Hong Kong-made smart-living technology, and quietly making the case that the buildings of tomorrow will be intelligent from the lobby up.

Why Innovation Parks Put Smart Mirrors on Display

Innovation parks run on demonstration. Researchers, investors, government officials, and prospective tenants walk through buildings like Building 11 every week, and every one of them is asking the same unspoken question: what does the future feel like here? A smart mirror answers that question in the most direct way possible — not with a brochure, but with an object you can touch, talk to, and use.

There is a reason the smart mirror works so well as a showcase piece. It is immediately understandable — everyone knows what a mirror is — and then it does something unexpected: it shows the time, weather, and news; it responds to voice; it connects to the smart home. That little arc from familiar to surprising is exactly what a technology demonstration should deliver. Visitors remember the mirror long after they have forgotten the slide deck.

Smart Mirrors Beyond the Home: Lobbies, Showrooms, Demo Suites

The Building 11 installation hints at a much bigger story: the smart mirror’s second career outside the home. Consider where an intelligent, always-on display earns its keep in a technology park. The reception lobby, where it welcomes visitors with the day’s agenda and park announcements. The showroom or demo suite, where it demonstrates Hong Kong smart-living technology to delegations. The executive lounge or talent apartment, where it serves residents the way it serves homeowners.

Each of these is a general scenario rather than a claim about one building — but the pattern is real and growing. Commercial and institutional spaces are discovering what hotels learned years ago: an information surface that is also a beautiful object beats a notice board or a bolted-on screen every time. And because the mirror needs no more space than a mirror, it fits lobbies and corridors that were never designed for displays.

For park operators, there is an additional dividend: showcasing local innovation. A Hong Kong-designed, Hong Kong-manufactured smart mirror — from a company with 20+ years in business and an own factory in Shenzhen — tells the park’s story better than imported gadgetry. It says the innovation ecosystem here produces finished products, not just prototypes.

From Pilot to Park-Wide: How Tech Campuses Adopt New Hardware

How does a single spotted mirror become a campus standard? Usually in stages. First, a pilot: one unit in a visible location — a lobby, a showroom, a talent apartment — where staff and visitors can experience it. Then evaluation: does it survive daily use, does anyone need to maintain it, do people actually use it? Then, if the answers are good, rollout: standardising across lobbies, meeting suites, and residential units.

The smart mirror is well suited to this path because the risk at each stage is low. A pilot is a single installation, not a building system. Maintenance is minimal — a sealed surface that wipes clean, with no moving parts. And usefulness is immediate: time, weather, announcements, and smart-home control are valuable from day one, with no training required. That is why “spotted at Building 11” is a meaningful milestone rather than a curiosity: pilots are how campuses decide.
