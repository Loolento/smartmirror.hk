Pano Harbour Smart Mirror Health Accessories Guide

June 30, 2025

Pano Harbour stands as a benchmark of luxury housing in Hong Kong, and its homes are equipped with high-tech Oolaa smart mirrors. Each mirror works with two health accessories — a skin analyzer and a body-fat scale — that make managing your health and beauty a simple part of the day. The video above shows the accessories in use; here is what each one does.

The skin analyzer: AI analysis, live moisture readings

The skin analyzer uses AI to analyze your skin, displaying its moisture level in real time. Instead of guessing how your skin is doing — or relying on how it happens to feel on a given morning — you get an actual reading, taken at the mirror you already use every day.

That changes how skincare decisions get made. A reading you can see, repeated day after day, tells you far more than a one-off impression, and it turns the mirror from a passive surface into an active part of a beauty routine.

The body-fat scale: weight, BMI and body fat, at a glance

The body-fat scale connects to the smart mirror and records your weight, BMI and body-fat percentage, with the data presented clearly — everything visible at a glance. There is no separate notebook to keep and no separate app to check; the record lives at the mirror, where you will actually see it.

Used together, the two accessories cover the two sides of personal health management that residents ask about most: how the body is doing, and how the skin is doing. Step on the scale, check your skin, and the numbers are there before you have finished getting ready.

A new standard for smart, healthy living

The Pano Harbour smart mirror pairs high technology with luxury living, setting a new standard for smart health at home. It reflects a broader shift in high-end residences: wellness features are no longer confined to the gym or the clubhouse. They are moving into the bathroom, into the daily routine, where they are used often enough to matter.

For residents, that is the real benefit. Health management stops being a separate chore — an appointment, a device in a drawer — and becomes something that happens naturally, in front of the mirror, every single morning.

It also helps that the two accessories ask so little of the user. There is no new routine to learn and no separate gadget to charge, carry or remember: stand where you always stand, and the mirror and its accessories do the recording for you. In a residence, that kind of invisibility is the difference between a feature that gets used for a week and one that is still being used years later.
