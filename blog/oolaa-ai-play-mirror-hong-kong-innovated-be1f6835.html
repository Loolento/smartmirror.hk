Oolaa Ai-Play Mirror: AR Interactive Gaming Mirror

Aug 11, 2025

The Oolaa Ai-Play Mirror is a Hong Kong-innovated relief mirror with AR/VR interactive gaming — a mirror designed not just to reflect you, but to play with you. This is part 2 of a six-part series introducing the Ai-Play Mirror's interactive games, and this instalment puts the spotlight on the Kung Fung Game. Stress relief, meet playtime: the mirror becomes the game board, and you become the controller.

What is the Ai-Play Mirror?

Most mirrors are passive: they show you yourself and nothing more. The Ai-Play Mirror breaks that contract. It is an interactive mirror with AR and VR gaming built in — the reflective surface doubles as a display, the camera tracks your movement, and augmented-reality game elements appear on the mirror for you to interact with using your own body. No controllers, no headsets, no learning curve: stand in front of the mirror and play.

The "relief" in its name is deliberate. This is a mirror designed for decompression — for the ten-minute break between meetings, the after-school wind-down, the waiting-room moment that would otherwise be spent scrolling a phone. Interactive play engages the body and the attention in a way passive screen time does not, which is exactly why it relieves stress rather than adding to it.

Part 2: the Kung Fung Game

Each part of this six-part series introduces one of the Ai-Play Mirror's interactive games. Part 2 features the Kung Fung Game — a motion-based game played through the mirror's AR interface. Like the other games in the series, it uses the mirror's camera and display to turn your movements into gameplay: what you do in front of the mirror is what happens in the game.

The design philosophy across the series is consistent: games that take seconds to understand, reward movement, and can be enjoyed in short bursts. That makes them suited to the places the Ai-Play Mirror is designed for — spaces where people wait, gather, or take breaks, and where a few minutes of active play is worth more than a few minutes of passive scrolling.

Why AR gaming on a mirror works

There is a reason the mirror is such a natural gaming surface. You already look at it — the feedback loop of seeing yourself inside the game is immediate and intuitive in a way a separate screen cannot match. When your reflection dodges, reaches or dances and the game responds, the connection between player and play is direct: no avatar stands between you and the action, because you are the avatar.

Removing controllers removes the biggest barrier to casual play. Anyone can walk up to the Ai-Play Mirror and start playing — children, grandparents, visitors who have never held a game controller. That accessibility is what makes it a relief mirror rather than a gaming device: it is there for whoever needs a moment of play, with zero setup and zero intimidation.

Where an interactive relief mirror belongs

Think about the spaces in your life built around waiting or decompressing. School activity rooms and student centres, where the Ai School Kiosk line already serves education settings. Gym and studio lobbies, where members arrive early for class. Clinic and salon waiting areas, where a few minutes of play beats a few minutes of anxiety. Office breakout zones, where teams need genuine breaks from screens. Residential clubhouses, where families gather at weekends.

In each of these settings, the Ai-Play Mirror does double duty: it is a striking design piece when idle — a beautiful mirror on the wall — and an interactive attraction the moment someone steps up to it. For venue operators, that combination of decor and engagement is rare.
