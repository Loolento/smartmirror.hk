New Era of Smart Homes: Matter & Voice Control

Apr 1, 2025

It is time to stop thinking of your smartphone as a remote control. With one simple button, your phone can become a personal assistant — totally hands-free. Add smooth voice control, especially Siri on the HomePod mini, and the smart home finally behaves the way it was always promised to.

Your phone as personal assistant, not remote control

The old smart home model was app-by-app, device-by-device: open one app for lights, another for the air conditioner, a third for the curtains. Every new device added another icon and another learning curve. The new era flips that model — one simple action on your phone, and the home responds as a coordinated whole.

The difference is intent versus instruction. You should not have to think about which app controls which device; you should say what you want — or press one button — and the home figures out the rest. Hands-free control is what makes a smart home feel smart instead of merely connected.

Matter: the standard that ends ecosystem wars

The biggest reason the old model failed was fragmentation. Devices bought into Apple's world would not talk to Google's, and a Xiaomi sensor lived in yet another silo. Matter, the industry-wide connectivity standard, was created to end that: certified devices work across ecosystems regardless of brand.

Oolaa's smart home lineup supports Matter alongside Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi and KNX — full protocol support, not a single-protocol compromise. Through Matter, an Oolaa smart mirror connects with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa and Xiaomi alike. For homeowners, that means buying for the home you have, not the ecosystem you are locked into. For developers, it means specifying technology that will not strand buyers in an obsolete platform five years after handover.

The timing matters. As Matter adoption grows across lighting, locks, sensors and appliances, the homes being specified today will live with these standards for a decade or more. Choosing Matter-compatible infrastructure now — including the mirror that will serve as the home's visible interface — is the closest thing the industry has to future-proofing. A mirror that speaks every major ecosystem will not need replacing when the household adds its next device, whatever brand it carries.

Voice control that feels natural

Voice is the interface that finally matches how people actually live — hands full, eyes busy, no desire to hunt for a phone. Siri on the HomePod mini delivers smooth, reliable voice control for the Apple household, while Google Assistant handles voice commands with Cantonese support, which matters enormously in Hong Kong.

For our friends in Hong Kong, being able to control the home in Cantonese — your mother language — is what turns voice control from a novelty into a daily habit. A command you can speak naturally is a command you will actually use; a command that forces you into a second language is one you will abandon. That is why Oolaa has invested for years in mature Cantonese voice command capability rather than treating it as a checkbox feature.

The mirror as the home's control surface

In this new era, the smart mirror earns its place as the home's visible control surface. Oolaa's Foyer mirror acts as a whole-home control hub via Google Assistant, Bluetooth and Zigbee — the thing you walk past, glance at and speak to, while the phone stays in your pocket.

Picture the scenario: one button on the phone as you leave, and the home settles itself — or a word in Cantonese at the mirror in the morning, and the day's weather, schedule and home status appear together. The smartphone becomes the assistant; the mirror becomes the dashboard. That is the new era of smart homes, and it is built on open standards, natural voice and surfaces that are already part of the home.
