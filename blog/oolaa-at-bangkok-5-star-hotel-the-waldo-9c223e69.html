Waldorf Astoria Bangkok: Oolaa Pivot Bathroom TV

Mar 16, 2026

An innovated Pivot Bathroom TV by Oolaa is now installed at the prestigious five-star hotel The Waldorf Astoria Bangkok, bringing a new level of smart living experience to the property. Featuring a unique pivoting screen that rotates left and right, guests can enjoy television from any angle — whether they are at the vanity, in the bath, or stepping out of the shower.

The problem with fixed bathroom screens

A bathroom is not a living room. Guests move through it — from the vanity to the bath to the shower — and a fixed screen only serves one of those positions well. Anyone who has craned their neck to watch a wall-mounted hotel TV from the bathtub knows the frustration: the picture is there, but the angle is wrong, the glare is wrong, and the experience falls short of the luxury the room promises.

This is a design problem, not a content problem. The television itself can be excellent, but if it cannot face the viewer, it might as well not be there. In a five-star bathroom, where every other detail has been considered, a screen locked to a single viewing angle is a conspicuous weak point.

How the pivoting screen changes the experience

The Oolaa Pivot Bathroom TV answers with a screen that rotates left and right. The guest simply turns the display toward wherever they are — the vanity while getting ready, the bathtub during a long soak, the shower area — and the television follows them around the room. It is a small mechanical idea with an outsized effect on comfort: the screen is always where you want it, at the angle you want it.

For a property like the Waldorf Astoria Bangkok, that flexibility is part of the luxury proposition. Five-star hospitality is about removing small frictions before the guest notices them, and a screen that turns to face you is exactly that kind of invisible thoughtfulness. It also photographs beautifully for the property's marketing — a visible, memorable amenity that differentiates the room in a competitive Bangkok luxury market.

Built for the bathroom, not adapted to it

A pivoting mechanism in a bathroom has to survive the bathroom: steam, humidity, temperature swings and daily use by guests who will not read instructions. Oolaa is a bathroom TV specialist — the market leader in Hong Kong bathroom TV and kitchen TV, with 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau — and its bathroom products are engineered for exactly this environment, built to IP65 with a built-in defogger, a higher protection rating than the IPX4 common in the category.

Ease of use matters just as much as durability. Hotel guests will not log in to anything or work through pairing menus, which is why Oolaa's flagship approach is "No Login. Just Cast." — content goes straight to the screen with no accounts and no fuss. A pivoting TV that is also effortless to use is one guests will actually enjoy, rather than admire once and ignore.

What hotel operators should consider

If you are specifying bathroom entertainment for a hotel, the Waldorf Astoria Bangkok installation suggests three questions to ask. First: can the guest actually see the screen from everywhere they will be — vanity, bath and shower? A pivoting screen answers yes in a way no fixed panel can. Second: is the product genuinely built for bathrooms — moisture rating, defogger, robust mechanics — or is it a living-room TV with a waterproof claim? Third: can guests use it with zero learning curve?
