Waldorf Astoria Bangkok: Oolaa Pivot Bathroom TV

Mar 16, 2026

An innovative Pivot Bathroom TV by Oolaa is now installed at the prestigious five-star hotel The Waldorf Astoria Bangkok, bringing a new level of smart living experience to the property. For a hotel of this standing, every detail of the guest room is scrutinized — and the bathroom is no exception.

A screen that turns to face you

The defining feature of the Pivot Bathroom TV is right there in its name: a unique pivoting screen that rotates left and right. That matters because bathroom time is never spent in a single spot. A guest might be soaking in the bathtub one moment and standing at the vanity the next, and a fixed screen can only serve one of those moments well.

The pivot serves both. Guests can enjoy television from any angle — whether they are in the bathtub or at the vanity area — simply by turning the screen toward where they are. It is a small mechanical idea that removes a daily friction most hotels never think about.

Redefining bathroom entertainment

Traditional bathroom entertainment, where it existed at all, meant a fixed screen, a fixed seat, and a compromise. The Oolaa Pivot Bathroom TV redefines that tradition: the entertainment adapts to the guest, not the other way around.

The result is a bathroom that seamlessly combines luxury and comfort. The Pivot Bathroom TV enhances the overall guest experience and elevates guests' sense of happiness — innovative smart living, applied to the room where every hotel day begins and ends.

What five-star hotels look for in a bathroom screen

For hotel teams specifying bathroom entertainment, the questions are usually practical. Can guests actually see the screen from the tub and from the vanity, or only from one of them? Does the fixture sit comfortably within a high-end bathroom design, rather than looking like a television that wandered in from the bedroom? And will it keep performing in a wet, steamy room, day after day, guest after guest?

A pivoting screen answers the first question in a way a fixed installation cannot. The broader lesson for buyers is to judge a bathroom TV the way a guest will experience it: from the water, at an angle, in the middle of a routine — not from a showroom wall.
