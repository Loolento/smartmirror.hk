How Oolaa Protects Mirrors During Bulk Delivery

May 19, 2024

For bulk order delivery, customers often enquire how we protect our products during delivery, given that smart mirrors are particularly susceptible to damage. A smart mirror is a sheet of glass with a display and electronics behind it — on a project order of hundreds of units, packaging is not an afterthought. It is part of the product. Here is how Oolaa packs every mirror for the journey from our factory to your site.

Individual box, wooden frame

When dispatching our products, each smart mirror is securely packaged in an individual cardboard box, which is then surrounded by a wooden frame for added protection. The logic is layered: the individual box holds and cushions the mirror as a single unit, and the wooden frame takes the knocks of transport and site handling that cardboard alone cannot.

Individual packaging matters on bulk orders. Mirrors are not shipped as one fragile stack; each unit travels as its own protected package, so a single impact does not cascade through the whole delivery, and units can be distributed floor by floor without being unpacked and re-handled more than necessary.

Labels that speak for the site team

Most importantly, we also affix labels to the wooden frame, displaying vital information required by the client, such as the dimensions of the smart mirror and the delivery unit. On a busy site, that label does real work: the receiving team can check a unit's size and destination at a glance, without opening the frame, and route each mirror to the right unit the first time.

Fewer opened frames means fewer chances for site damage — and when installation day comes, the mirror that arrives at each door is the mirror that was specified for it.

Custom packaging for project requirements

Even more importantly, we are also able to customise the design of each type of packaging according to the specific requirements of our clients. Different projects handle deliveries differently — hoist sizes, storage space and installation sequences vary — so the packaging can be designed around the project rather than forcing the project to work around standard packing.

That is the complete picture: individual cardboard packaging, a protective wooden frame, client information labelled on the outside, and a packaging design that can be customised when a project needs it. It is how a fragile, high-value product survives bulk delivery — by treating protection as engineering, not wrapping.
