Oolaa Smart Mirror Inside Mount PokFuLam Homes

February 3, 2025

Oolaa is involved in some of the most luxurious residential projects in Hong Kong — and Mount PokFuLam, developed by Wang On Properties, sits near the top of that list. With each residence priced at a minimum of US$40 million, every detail is scrutinised. Each master bathroom features the innovative Oolaa Smart Mirror, specified with a unique and flamboyant design that matches the ambition of the development itself.

When the brief is ultra-luxury

At the US$40-million level, a bathroom is not a utility room — it is a private gallery. Stone is book-matched, taps are sculptural, and lighting is choreographed. Into this environment, technology must arrive quietly. A plastic gadget on the wall would be a failure no matter how clever its software; the technology has to earn its place visually before it earns it functionally.

This is precisely the test the Oolaa Smart Mirror was designed to pass. When off, it is a beautifully finished mirror — in the Mount PokFuLam master bathrooms, a statement piece with a distinctive, flamboyant design that holds its own against marble and brass. When on, it becomes an information surface: time, weather, music, and more, displayed on the glass itself. Nothing is bolted on, nothing clutters the vanity, and nothing asks the interior designer to compromise.

For Wang On Properties, specifying Oolaa meant specifying a product that the design team could embrace rather than work around. That distinction — technology as a design material, not a design problem — is what separates a luxury smart home from an expensive gadget collection.

The master bathroom as a technology stage

Why does the smart mirror belong in the master bathroom? Because the bathroom hosts the two most information-hungry moments of the day: the morning routine and the evening wind-down. In the morning, the mirror can show the weather, the calendar, and the news while you shave or apply makeup. In the evening, it becomes ambient — music, soft lighting scenes, a moment of calm. No other room concentrates so much daily ritual into so few square metres.

Oolaa's bathroom mirrors are engineered for this exact environment. The mirror carries an IP65 rating — a higher level of water and dust protection than competing products such as ORVIBO's IPX4 — along with a built-in defogger that keeps the glass clear after a hot shower, and motion detection that can wake the display as you approach. These are not luxury extras; in a Hong Kong bathroom, with its humidity and temperature swings, they are the difference between a mirror that works for years and one that fails in months.

Voice control completes the picture. Through Google Assistant — with Cantonese supported — the mirror responds to spoken commands, so wet hands never need to touch a screen. It is a small detail that reveals a large design principle: the technology adapts to the bathroom, not the other way around.

What developers gain from a smart mirror partner

For developers working at the luxury tier, the smart mirror decision is really three decisions: the product, the partner, and the aftercare. The product must satisfy the design team. The partner must handle specification, custom sizing, and site coordination without drama. And the aftercare must reassure buyers who expect decades of service from a US$40-million home.

Oolaa answers all three from a single address. As a Hong Kong company with its own factory in Shenzhen — factory-direct, not a trading company — it can produce custom sizes and finishes to match a project's joinery and stone. With more than 31,000 installations across Hong Kong and Macau and 20+ years in business, it brings a track record that few smart home brands in the region can match. And as the market leader in Hong Kong bathroom TV and kitchen TV, it understands the bathroom environment better than any generalist electronics brand.

The company's project history reinforces the point: Oolaa has supplied bathroom TV and smart mirror solutions to Sun Hung Kai (SHK) developments among others. When the city's most demanding developers keep specifying the same partner, it is because the partner keeps delivering.

The technology behind the mirror

Strip away the luxury framing and the engineering is straightforward: a high-quality mirror bonded to a display, sealed against moisture, with a defogger element behind the glass, motion sensing, and smart home connectivity. Oolaa's platform supports Matter, Zigbee, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and KNX, and connects with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, and Xiaomi through Matter — so the bathroom mirror can participate in whole-home scenes, from a "good morning" routine to an evening wind-down.

The "No Login. Just Cast." philosophy runs through the product line: guests and family members can use the mirror's smart features without accounts, passwords, or app downloads. In a luxury residence — where the technology should feel effortless — that zero-friction approach is exactly right.

Mount PokFuLam shows what happens when a developer, a design team, and a technology partner all aim at the same target. The result is a master bathroom where the mirror is both a design statement and a quiet piece of infrastructure — flamboyant in appearance, invisible in operation.
