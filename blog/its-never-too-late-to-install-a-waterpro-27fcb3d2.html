Waterproof Bathroom TV: It's Never Too Late

August 12, 2022

As a child, I remember my parents sending me off for a shower during the commercial break of our favourite TV programme — and every bath ended in a rush. Technology has fixed that small frustration: a waterproof TV in the bathroom means the programme no longer has to wait, and whether your bathroom is brand new or twenty years old, it is genuinely never too late to install one.

Why Hong Kong Homes Are Made for Bathroom TVs

Hong Kong living comes with a particular rhythm: long working hours, compact flats, and a bathroom that is often the only room where you get twenty uninterrupted minutes to yourself. Missing the evening news, a live football match, or the next episode of a drama series because you needed a shower is a small but real daily annoyance — the same one I remember from childhood, when commercial breaks dictated bath time.

A bathroom TV changes that equation: morning news while you shave, a drama episode during a long soak, music while you get ready for work. For households where the living-room TV is always occupied, the bathroom screen becomes a personal one — that little touch of hotel luxury, now entirely achievable at home.

There is a practical side too: parents can follow a programme while bathing young children nearby, and elderly family members gain entertainment plus useful information like the time and weather at a glance.

What “Waterproof” Really Means: IP Ratings Explained

Not every TV belongs in a bathroom. Steam, splashes, and constant humidity will destroy an ordinary television in short order — which is why the single most important specification is the IP (ingress protection) rating. A proper bathroom TV should be sealed against water jets and moisture from every direction.

Oolaa bathroom mirror TVs are rated IP65: fully protected against dust and against water jets from any angle. To put that in perspective, that is a higher rating than well-known alternatives such as ORVIBO’s IPX4 products. IP65 means the screen can live happily beside your shower for years, not months. It also means the electronics are protected against the fine, penetrating steam that is the real enemy in a bathroom — far more damaging than the occasional splash.

Beyond the rating, look for a built-in defogger — a heated demisting layer that keeps the display clear in a steamy bathroom — and motion detection that wakes the screen as you enter. Together they make the TV behave like part of the room rather than an appliance fighting it.

Retrofitting an Existing Bathroom Is Easier Than You Think

The most common objection we hear is: “My bathroom is already finished — isn’t it too late?” In most cases, no. A mirror TV replaces your existing bathroom mirror, so it needs no new wall space and no rethinking of the layout. The display hides behind a genuine mirror surface; when the screen is off, it is simply the mirror you already had.

Consider typical scenarios: a twenty-year-old flat in Kowloon gets a light renovation, and while the electrician is on site a power point is routed to the mirror position — a 21.5-inch or 32-inch mirror TV goes up in an afternoon. A homeowner in a new development swaps the developer’s plain mirror for a smart version with news, weather, and streaming: same fixing points, same footprint.

The key is professional installation: waterproofing is only as good as the sealing and the electrical connection, so this is a job for a specialist team, not a weekend DIY project. A Quick Buyer’s Checklist for Bathroom TVs

If you are shopping for a bathroom TV, run through this checklist. First, confirm a genuine waterproof rating — IP65 or equivalent — and ask how the unit is sealed, not just what the brochure claims. Second, choose the right size: in a compact Hong Kong bathroom you will be one to two metres from the mirror, so 21.5 to 32 inches covers most cases.

Third, insist on a defogger; without one, the screen is useless for half the year. Frequently asked questions

Can I install a waterproof TV in an old, already-finished bathroom?

Yes, in most cases. A mirror TV mounts where your existing mirror is, so the layout does not change; an electrician can usually route a safe power connection during even a light renovation. Professional installation is essential in the wet environment.

What is the difference between a bathroom TV and a mirror TV?

A mirror TV is a bathroom TV whose display sits behind a genuine mirror surface. When the screen is off, it works as an ordinary mirror; when on, the picture shines through. This dual function is why it fits bathrooms so well — it adds a screen without sacrificing mirror space.

Is it safe to have a television in the bathroom?

A properly rated unit, professionally installed, is designed exactly for this environment. Look for IP65 sealing, proper electrical isolation, and installation by a specialist team experienced with wet-zone fittings — never install an ordinary TV in a bathroom yourself.

How much maintenance does a bathroom TV need?

Very little. The sealed design keeps moisture out, the defogger prevents fogging automatically, and the mirror surface cleans like any normal mirror. There are no filters to change and no special servicing schedule.
