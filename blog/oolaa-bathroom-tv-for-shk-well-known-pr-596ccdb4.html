Oolaa Bathroom TV in SHK's The Cullinan, Hong Kong

Mar 22, 2023

When Sun Hung Kai — the developer Hong Kong holds to the highest standard — features a product prominently in a flagship project, the industry notices. At The Cullinan by Kowloon Station, SHK put the Oolaa bathroom TV front and center: a waterproof design with a built-in DMB digital tuner, made for the most demanding room in a luxury home. It is about as clear an endorsement as this industry gives, and it confirms Oolaa's standing as Hong Kong's bathroom TV specialist.

Why a top developer puts a TV in the bathroom

The bathroom is the one room where a resident is guaranteed unhurried time, twice a day, with eyes free and hands busy. The morning news while shaving. A drama episode in the bath. Market data before the commute. A TV in the bathroom is not an extravagance layered onto luxury — it is luxury recognizing where attention actually lives.

For a developer of SHK's calibre, specifying a bathroom TV is also a statement of confidence. Bathrooms destroy ordinary electronics: steam, condensation, and splashes are not edge cases, they are the climate. Featuring the Oolaa bathroom TV prominently at The Cullinan tells buyers that every detail of the residence — including the one most developers leave as an empty wall — was considered. Oolaa has supplied SHK projects before; this is what a track record buys.

Waterproof by design, not by afterthought

There are two ways to make electronics survive a bathroom. The common way is to take a standard product and seal it up afterward — gaskets, coatings, crossed fingers. The Oolaa way is to design for the wet room from the first sketch: sealed construction, moisture-proof circuitry, and materials chosen for a lifetime of steam. The result is a waterproof design that treats the bathroom as its natural habitat rather than a hazard to survive.

That philosophy runs through Oolaa's whole bathroom range — its bathroom mirrors are built to IP65 waterproofing with defoggers and motion detection, because the company assumes the worst the room can offer and engineers past it. A bathroom TV from a bathroom specialist is simply a different product from a TV that happens to be moisture-resistant.

The built-in DMB digital tuner: live TV, no boxes

The Cullinan installation carries a built-in DMB digital tuner — which means live television with no set-top box, no streaming stick, and no extra wiring snaking through the wall. Switch on, watch the news. For a bathroom, that simplicity is everything: nobody wants to pair a remote with wet hands or troubleshoot a streaming login before the morning headlines.

Broadcast TV also sidesteps the two quiet failures of bathroom streaming — patchy bathroom Wi-Fi and subscription logins on a shared household screen. The tuner just works, for every resident and every guest, the way television did before everything needed an account. In a luxury residence, “it just works” is the whole brief.

What to specify in a luxury bathroom

If you are fitting out a high-end bathroom — as a developer, a hotel operator, or a homeowner — judge a bathroom TV on four things. First, genuine waterproofing engineered for steam and splashes, not a converted living-room panel. Second, the content source: a built-in tuner for live TV removes boxes, remotes, and logins from the wettest room in the house. Third, integration: Oolaa's range extends to mirror TVs, so the screen can vanish into the mirror when off — one surface, two functions.

Fourth, and most overlooked: who answers when something goes wrong in year three. Oolaa is a Hong Kong company (Viewdex Limited) with its own factory in Shenzhen — factory-direct, not a trading company — with 31,000+ installations across Hong Kong and Macau.
