Oolaa Way Vs Old Way Mirror TV

For two decades, hotel mirror TVs have been built the same way: a display panel with a separate Android set-top box stuffed behind the wall. It works — until it doesn't.

Oolaa took a different path. Here's how they compare, side by side.

HardwareSeparate Android box behind wallEmbedded module inside mirror frame

Why Hotel Engineers Prefer Oolaa

The wall cavity is the enemy. Behind every hotel mirror, there's a war being fought against heat, dust, moisture, and inaccessible wiring. The old Android-box approach puts a heat-generating computer into that hostile environment and hopes for the best.

Oolaa's embedded design removes the set-top box entirely. Processing, power management, and connectivity are all integrated into the mirror frame — accessible from the front, cooled by the mirror surface itself.

The result: fewer failure points, simpler installation, and a product that ages gracefully.

The Real Cost

Set-top box mirror TVs look cheaper on the BOM. But factor in the extra electrical work, the larger wall cavity, the shorter lifespan, and the maintenance calls — and Oolaa comes out ahead within the first two years of operation. Hotels that have switched report 40% fewer service calls on mirror TVs after adopting Oolaa.
